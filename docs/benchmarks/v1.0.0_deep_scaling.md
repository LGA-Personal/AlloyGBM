## Competitiveness smoke baseline

On 2026-09-04 PDT (America/Los_Angeles), the pinned pre-optimization commit
`adfa2c8e593cea68b124e7975f3b4fd9f862a148` was measured with the six-scenario
`pr_smoke.yaml` matrix at one thread, one warmup, and five timed repetitions:

```text
python -m benchmarks.competitiveness.run \
  --manifest benchmarks/competitiveness/manifests/pr_smoke.yaml \
  --output-dir /tmp/alloygbm-pr-baseline \
  --threads 1 --repetitions 5 --warmups 1
```

The capture ran on `Logans-MacBook-Air-4.local` (Apple M4, 10 logical CPUs,
Darwin arm64) with Python 3.13.5. Recorded package versions were AlloyGBM
1.0.0, LightGBM 4.6.0, XGBoost 3.2.0, and CatBoost 1.2.10. The immutable
artifacts are [raw JSONL](../../benchmarks/competitiveness/baselines/adfa2c8-pr-smoke.jsonl)
and [the derived summary](../../benchmarks/competitiveness/baselines/adfa2c8-pr-smoke.summary.json),
with [run metadata](../../benchmarks/competitiveness/baselines/adfa2c8-pr-smoke.run-metadata.json)
binding the raw run to the measured and harness commits plus the manifest checksum.
The summary uses ordinary median and unscaled MAD over all five repetitions,
not the older report's best-of-two timing.

This is a structural and provenance cross-check only. Its dense fixture is
4,096×40 with 40 rounds and depth 6; the deep-scaling report starts with
different, much larger configurations, so these timings and metrics are not
numerically comparable. Both captures used the same observed Darwin arm64
Apple M4 host family; the smoke run records the exact hostname and machine
metadata in every raw record. Existing deep-scaling tables above are retained
unchanged.

## Published dense-deep cross-check

The exact published tall fixture was also rerun at the pinned pre-optimization
commit, using the generator semantics from
`benchmarks/deep_scaling_comparison.py`: seed `20260902`, 500,000 rows × 40
features, an 80/20 split, 200 rounds, depth 8, learning rate 0.05, row and
column subsampling 0.8, 256 bins, one thread, one warmup, and five timed
repetitions. The immutable [raw records](../../benchmarks/competitiveness/baselines/adfa2c8-published-v1-crosscheck.jsonl),
[median/MAD summary](../../benchmarks/competitiveness/baselines/adfa2c8-published-v1-crosscheck.summary.json),
and [run metadata](../../benchmarks/competitiveness/baselines/adfa2c8-published-v1-crosscheck.run-metadata.json)
identify the measured AlloyGBM commit, harness revision, and manifest checksum.

The cross-check produced median fit time **15.9686s ± 0.1200s MAD** and
median test RMSE **0.16377461**. The published table reports best-of-two fit
time 16.76s and RMSE 0.16378 for this same case, so this capture is 4.722%
faster on median and has 0.00329% lower RMSE. The RMSE agreement is effectively
exact; the modest timing delta is consistent with comparing a five-repetition
median against a best-of-two observation on the same Apple M4/Darwin arm64
host. These are numerical results for the matching fixture, while the smoke
baseline above remains a separate structural/provenance cross-check.

Companion to `v1.0.0_comparison.md`, which measures a curated accuracy suite at
one thread budget. This sweeps the thread budget across deep, many-tree
configurations to characterise *scaling*.

## Fairness

One thread budget per run, applied to each library's own knob and to the
OpenMP/BLAS environment. Matched rounds, depth, learning rate, seed, bin count
(256), row subsample (0.8) and column subsample (0.8); LightGBM gets
`subsample_freq=1` (it ignores `subsample` otherwise) and `num_leaves=2**depth`
(its default of 31 would cap it far below the depth-wise peers). Each budget
runs in its own subprocess so the thread environment is pinned before import.
Best of 2 repeats.

AlloyGBM's auto training policy can silently override an explicitly set
`row_subsample`, so the harness reads back `resolved_training_policy_` after
every fit and records whether it matched the peers. **All 216 cells matched.**

Two caveats on capacity. LightGBM grows leaf-wise and stops when gains are
exhausted, so at `depth=12` it may build far fewer than 2^12 nodes; AlloyGBM
and XGBoost both grow depth-wise, making that the closer like-for-like pair.
CatBoost grows oblivious trees, a different model class, and its `depth` is
capped at 16.

## Reading

**AlloyGBM is the slowest of the four at one thread in all nine
configurations**, by 1.4x to 3.6x. This is the headline finding and it is a
per-core throughput gap, not a parallelism one.

**AlloyGBM's scaling is the best or second-best in seven of nine
configurations** (2.3x–4.5x from 1 to 10 threads), which closes much of that
gap but rarely overtakes. At 10 threads AlloyGBM is fastest in no
configuration, though it beats LightGBM in three and XGBoost in one.

**LightGBM degrades with more threads on deep trees.** At 200 rounds / depth 12
on 500k x 40 it runs 23.7 s on one thread and 47.9 s on ten — twice as slow
with ten times the cores (0.49x). The same reversal appears on 1M x 60
(0.89x) and, more mildly, in its depth-8 rows, where its best time is at 2–4
threads. On this host LightGBM's optimum is around 4 threads and adding more
hurts; the 4-performance/6-efficiency core split is the likely cause, since a
straggling efficiency core delays every join.

**XGBoost is the strongest overall at high thread counts**, and is bit-exact
across thread counts (see the portability review). Exactness and speed are not
in tension.

**On accuracy, CatBoost wins all nine cells** and AlloyGBM is last or
second-last in most. Read this narrowly: it is a single synthetic generator
(linear signal plus one interaction term) at each library's own defaults for
everything not matched above, and CatBoost's ordered boosting suits it well.
`v1.0.0_comparison.md`'s curated suite remains the accuracy authority. It is
still worth noting that AlloyGBM's RMSE trails LightGBM's by roughly 5-8% here,
which is not explained by capacity, since capacity was matched.
