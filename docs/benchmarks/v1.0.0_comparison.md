## Fairness controls

Every library is given the same work and the same resources. Each run records
its settings in the JSON output under `params.fairness` and
`params.environment`.

- **Equal compute budget.** `--threads N` is applied through each library's own
  knob (AlloyGBM/LightGBM/XGBoost `n_jobs`, CatBoost `thread_count`), and
  `OMP_NUM_THREADS`, `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS`,
  `NUMEXPR_NUM_THREADS`, and `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS` are pinned to the same
  value so no runtime quietly takes more.
- **Equal hyperparameters.** Shared `n_estimators`, `learning_rate`,
  `max_depth`, seed, and 0.8/0.8 row/column subsampling. Two corrections are
  required for that equality to be real: LightGBM ignores `subsample` unless
  `subsample_freq >= 1`, and its `num_leaves` default of 31 would cap trees
  below the depth-wise peers' `2 ** max_depth`. Both are set explicitly.
- **Equal data.** The same arrays and the same split for every library; data
  preparation and metric computation are outside the timed region.

> **Re-measured 2026-09-05.** The tables below supersede the 2026-08-31 run.
> Peer numbers are unchanged within noise (median fit-time ratio 0.997–1.003
> against the earlier run), which is what makes the AlloyGBM movement
> attributable: its curated fit times fell 3–15% and its 1M-row
> multi-threaded fits by about 40%. Accuracy is unchanged — every AlloyGBM
> metric in the curated suite is identical to the earlier run, because the
> intervening changes were verified byte-identical at the artifact level.
>
> One thing the superseded data recorded that the new data does not: AlloyGBM's
> 1M-row metrics varied with thread count (binary accuracy 0.66343 at one
> thread against 0.663665 at ten). That was a real determinism defect, fixed
> before this run; all four libraries are now stable across thread counts in
> `data/v1.0.0_scale.json`. The earlier file is preserved in git history rather
> than deleted, since it is the evidence the defect existed.

> **Pre-1.0 results are not comparable.** Before v1.0.0 the harness pinned the
> peer libraries to a single thread while leaving AlloyGBM unconstrained, so it
> used every core. Any speed comparison from an earlier run is inflated by
> roughly AlloyGBM's parallel speedup. The accuracy claims also shifted once
> LightGBM's bagging and leaf capacity were corrected.

## Why the curated suite is single-threaded

The curated scenarios span 142 to 40,000 rows. At that size, giving every
library 10 threads measures thread-spawn overhead rather than throughput: on
the reference host, LightGBM and XGBoost are **slower** multi-threaded than
single-threaded below roughly 40,000 rows — LightGBM by up to 20x on the
smallest scenarios.

| Scenario | Rows | AlloyGBM | LightGBM | XGBoost | CatBoost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `wine_multiclass` | 142 | 0.97x | 0.06x | 0.06x | 3.23x |
| `dense_numeric` | 1,279 | 1.82x | 0.07x | 0.22x | 1.87x |
| `panel_time_series` | 5,618 | 1.28x | 0.16x | 0.39x | 2.42x |
| `california_housing` | 16,512 | 1.46x | 0.18x | 0.57x | 1.72x |
| `histogram_stress` | 40,000 | 2.17x | 0.64x | 1.96x | 3.41x |

*(fit-time speedup from 1 thread to 10 threads; below 1.0 means threading made
that library slower)*

Reporting a multi-threaded win at this scale would flatter AlloyGBM for a
reason unrelated to its algorithms. The suite is therefore run
single-threaded — a clean per-core comparison that is also the most
reproducible across machines — and the realistic multi-threaded case is
measured separately at 200k and 1M rows.

Synthetic regression and binary classification, all four libraries at the same

## What the results say

**Accuracy.** Across the 15 curated scenarios AlloyGBM wins 5 and places in the
top two on 11. It is strongest on `histogram_stress` (0.180 RMSE against
0.360–0.365 for all three peers — a 2x margin), `bike_sharing`,
`dow_jones_financial`, and both ranking scenarios. It trails on
`panel_time_series` (last of four, 4.3% behind CatBoost),
`california_housing`, `breast_cancer`, and `adult_income`. At 200k–1M rows,
accuracy is effectively tied with all three peers.

**Fit speed.** This is AlloyGBM's weak axis, and the gap has two distinct
causes:

- *Fixed per-round overhead* dominates on small data. On the curated suite
  (median 5.6k rows) LightGBM fits about 3.2x faster per core and XGBoost
  about 3.0x. At 200k rows that shrinks to roughly 1.3x, and at 1M rows to
  about 1.5x — the per-row work is competitive; the constant is not.
- *Single-thread throughput* is now the larger gap, and it is the honest
  headline. Parallel scaling used to be: at v1.0.0's first measurement
  AlloyGBM reached only 1.6–1.8x on 10 cores against LightGBM's 2.5–2.8x. It
  now reaches 2.43–2.58x against LightGBM's 2.81–2.98x and CatBoost's
  3.35–3.38x, which is within the same band rather than a category behind.
  What did not move is per-core speed, so that is where the remaining
  difference lives (see [limitations](../limitations.md)). XGBoost still shows
  no scaling on this host and workload (0.97–1.00x), so AlloyGBM is roughly
  2x faster than XGBoost multi-threaded at 1M rows despite being slower
  single-threaded.

**Prediction speed.** Competitive: comparable to LightGBM and XGBoost at scale
and markedly faster than LightGBM single-threaded on the curated suite
(median 4.4x). CatBoost's oblivious trees predict fastest by a wide margin.

## Effect of the v1.0.0 default changes

Two v1.0.0 default changes were validated on this suite rather than asserted:

- `lambdarank_normalize=True` (matching LightGBM's `lambdarank_norm`) lifted
  `california_ranking` NDCG@10 from 0.6547 to **0.7674**, moving AlloyGBM from
  last to second and ahead of both LightGBM (0.7430) and CatBoost (0.7461).
  On uniform 50-document groups it improved NDCG@10 from 0.9649 to 0.9697.
- `lambdarank_truncation_level=30` (also matching LightGBM) is a
  speed-for-quality trade on datasets with large query groups. On
  `california_ranking` (median 120, max 3,307 documents per query), raising it
  to `None` gains a further ~0.04 NDCG@10 at ~1.5x the fit time. Users with
  large groups should consider raising it.
